Naga Pork with Bamboo Shoot

Official KatoriCalorie Recipe & Nutrient Profile

In the mountainous regions of Nagaland, traditional cooking is defined by woodfire smoke, zero commercial oils, and intense local aromatics. One of the most famous dishes of this culinary region is Naga Pork with Fermented Bamboo Shoot (বাঁহৰ গাজৰে সৈতে গাহৰি মাংস).

Unlike continental pork preparations that are heavily fried, breaded, or glazed in sugary sauces, this traditional Naga dish is boiled slowly in its own juices. The acidity of the fermented bamboo shoot cuts through the richness of the meat, tenderizing it naturally while infusing the broth with gut-friendly organic acids.

Step-by-Step Oil-Free Cooking Guide

Follow this clean cooking method to recreate this high-protein traditional dish:

  1. Select the Cut: Choose a mix of lean pork loin and slightly marbled cuts. Dice them into medium cubes. Wash and pat dry.
  2. Initial Cooking (No Oil): Place the meat directly into a heavy-bottomed pot. Add a pinch of salt and a cup of water. Cover and cook over low-to-medium heat. The pork will begin to cook in its own fat as it melts.
  3. Add Aromatics: Grind a large chunk of ginger and a bulb of garlic into a coarse paste. Toss this into the pot along with chopped Naga chilies (Raja Mircha / Ghost Pepper) to taste. Be extremely careful with the chili quantity!
  4. Introduce Bamboo Shoot: Add 2–3 tablespoons of wet fermented bamboo shoot (shredded). Stir well to distribute the tangy flavor.
  5. Simmer to Tenderness: Add another cup of hot water, cover, and let it simmer on low heat for 35–45 minutes. The water should reduce to a rich, aromatic gravy.
  6. Serve: Once the meat is tender and the fat has separated into a thin layer on top, garnish with wild coriander. Serve hot with a steaming mound of parboiled rice.

Dietary Strategy & Cholesterol Management

While pork is an outstanding source of complete protein, B-vitamins, and zinc, it is higher in saturated fats than poultry or fish.

To manage your blood lipid profile and stay within your calorie targets:

  • Portion Control: Limit your portion to 1 standard serving (180g) to keep fat intake under 21g.
  • Balance the Plate: Offset the fat content by filling the rest of your meal with dietary fiber—steamed local herbs, boiled greens, or raw cucumber slices.
