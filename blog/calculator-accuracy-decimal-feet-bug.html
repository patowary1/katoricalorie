Decimal Height Input: Understanding Feet-and-Inches Conversion Ambiguity

By the KatoriCalorie Editorial Board | Published June 2026

Online calorie calculators provide energy estimates to help guide nutrition planning. However, when user interfaces accept height in decimal feet, ambiguity between decimal fractions and feet-and-inches notation can introduce unexpected arithmetic discrepancies.

This issue arises from notation confusion: the Decimal Height Bug. In this article, we explain the mathematics behind this discrepancy, how it affects estimated Basal Metabolic Rate (BMR) and Total Daily Energy Expenditure (TDEE), and why KatoriCalorie uses direct metric inputs to avoid conversion ambiguity.

1. The Decimal Feet vs. Feet-and-Inches Dilemma

A decimal-feet input may display or accept a value such as 5.5 feet.

Decimal feet and feet-and-inches are different unit representations. To convert the fractional part of decimal feet to inches, multiply it by 12. For example, 5.5 decimal feet is calculated as:

5 feet + (0.5 * 12 inches) = 5 feet 6 inches (66 inches / 167.64 cm)

However, confusion occurs when a decimal value is erroneously interpreted as feet and inches:

Erroneous interpretation: 5.5 = 5 feet 5 inches (65 inches / 165.10 cm)

This creates a discrepancy of 1 full inch (2.54 cm). When an equation receives 165.10 cm instead of 167.64 cm, the resulting calculation reflects the incorrect physical dimension.

2. How a 1-Inch Discrepancy Affects Mifflin-St Jeor Math

Most high-quality calculators utilize the clinical gold-standard Mifflin-St Jeor formula to calculate Basal Metabolic Rate (BMR):

BMR (Men) = 10 * Weight(kg) + 6.25 * Height(cm) - 5 * Age(years) + 5

If a calculator incorrectly registers a user's height as 165.10 cm instead of 167.64 cm (a 1-inch error), the math changes as follows:

  • Underestimation of BMR: The calculation loses 6.25 * 2.54 = 15.875 kcal/day of energy allowance.
  • Underestimation of TDEE: Across standard physical activity multipliers (1.2 to 1.725), this shifts daily expenditure by approximately 19.05 to 27.38 kcal/day (24.61 kcal/day at moderate activity 1.55).

Similarly, for 5.6 feet (67.2 inches / 170.69 cm) misread as 5 feet 6 inches (66.0 inches / 167.64 cm), the discrepancy is 1.2 inches (3.048 cm), shifting BMR by 19.05 kcal/day and TDEE by 22.86 to 32.86 kcal/day.

3. Why Input Consistency Matters in Nutrition Planning

In the 1-inch (2.54 cm) example above, the calculated TDEE difference is approximately 19–27 kcal/day across KatoriCalorie's current activity multipliers. While this shift is modest, starting from a consistent baseline in structured meal planning helps avoid compounding uncertainties. This is especially relevant when budgeting regional dishes alongside staple foods—for instance, balancing traditional dishes or portioning items like Bihu pithas.

While metabolic calculations are always estimations rather than physiological certainties, eliminating known interface ambiguities ensures that your daily targets reflect deliberate planning rather than avoidable unit conversion errors.

4. Metric Inputs for Consistent Calculation

To prevent decimal-conversion ambiguity, KatoriCalorie does not use decimal feet sliders.

Instead, we ask users to input their height in centimeters (cm). Centimeters are straightforward metric units that avoid fractional unit confusion. The Mifflin-St Jeor equation runs directly on the entered centimeter value. While metabolic formulas inherently provide estimates rather than exact physiological measurements, direct metric inputs keep the baseline consistent. This same attention to consistency applies when comparing daily diet staples like roti and rice in our database.

5. Consistent Metric Calculation

The Mifflin-St Jeor equation uses height in centimeters. Accepting height directly in centimeters avoids an additional feet-and-inches conversion step and keeps the calculator input consistent. All BMR and TDEE formulas inherently remain estimates, but direct metric inputs eliminate preventable unit conversion ambiguity.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

Q: Why do some BMR calculators produce height conversion discrepancies?

Some calculators use ambiguous decimal sliders. For example, 5.6 feet is 67.2 inches (170.69 cm), but interpreting it as 5 feet 6 inches treats it as 66.0 inches (167.64 cm). This 1.2-inch (3.048 cm) discrepancy alters Mifflin-St Jeor BMR by 19.05 kcal/day and daily TDEE by approximately 23–33 kcal/day across typical activity levels.

Q: How does KatoriCalorie prevent height conversion ambiguity in BMR calculation?

Our platform takes height directly in centimeters (cm) and applies the Mifflin-St Jeor equation without decimal-feet conversion steps. This eliminates fractional unit ambiguity, though all BMR and TDEE values remain mathematical estimates.

Q: What happens if 5.5 decimal feet is misread as 5 feet 5 inches?

Decimal 5.5 feet equals 5 feet 6 inches (167.64 cm). If 5.5 is misread as 5 feet 5 inches (165.10 cm), it creates a height discrepancy of 1.0 inch (2.54 cm). Under Mifflin-St Jeor, this shifts estimated BMR by 15.875 kcal/day and TDEE by approximately 19–27 kcal/day across typical activity levels.

Q: Why does KatoriCalorie use centimeters (cm) for height input?

The Mifflin-St Jeor equation uses centimeters directly. By accepting height in centimeters, KatoriCalorie avoids unit conversion ambiguity between decimal feet and feet-and-inches notation, though all metabolic formulas remain estimates.
