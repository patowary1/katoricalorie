The Science of Fermented Foods in Northeast India

By the KatoriCalorie Editorial Board | Scientific Review of Indigenous Probiotic Preparations

In modern wellness spaces, fermented products like kombucha, kefir, and kimchi are highly celebrated. Yet, some of the world's most complex and therapeutically active fermented foods have been quietly prepared for centuries in the home kitchens of Northeast India. From the hills of Nagaland to the valleys of Assam and Sikkim, fermentation is not just a preservation technique; it is a metabolic necessity.

Traditional preparations like Axone, Gundruk, and fermented Bamboo Shoot are rich sources of beneficial bacteria, enzymes, and bioactive peptides. In this article, we analyze the micro-biology and nutritional benefits of these regional staples.

1. Axone (Akhuni): The Fermented Soybean Powerhouse

A cornerstone of Naga cuisine, Axone is made by boiling, wrapping, and fermenting whole soybeans. The fermentation is driven primarily by spore-forming bacilli, particularly Bacillus subtilis strains.

  • Probiotic Action: Axone serves as a rich source of live, spore-forming bacteria that can survive the acidic environment of the stomach, successfully colonizing the lower intestine.
  • Bioactive Peptides: During fermentation, proteins are broken down into free amino acids and peptides. These peptides have been shown in laboratory studies to possess anti-hypertensive and antioxidant properties.
  • Vitamin K2 (Menaquinone): Fermented soybeans are one of the few rich vegetarian sources of Vitamin K2, which is critical for directing calcium to the bones and keeping it out of the arterial walls.

2. Gundruk: The Fermented Leafy Greens

Popular across Sikkim, Darjeeling, and parts of Assam, Gundruk is prepared by crushing and fermenting leafy greens—usually mustard, radish, or cauliflower leaves—in airtight earthenware containers.

The lactic acid bacteria (LAB) involved, such as Lactobacillus plantarum and Lactobacillus brevis, ferment the sugars present in the leaves, producing lactic acid. This process:

  1. Reduces Anti-Nutrients: Raw leafy greens contain oxalates and phytates that bind to minerals, reducing their absorption. Fermentation breaks down these anti-nutrients, significantly increasing the bioavailability of calcium, iron, and magnesium.
  2. Improves Gut Barrier Function: The organic acids produced in Gundruk nourish the colonocytes (cells lining the colon) and support a healthy mucosal barrier, reducing gut permeability.

3. Fermented Bamboo Shoot (Khorisa)

In Assam, fermented bamboo shoot is known as Khorisa, while in other states it is referred to by regional names like Soibum or Bestanga. Young bamboo shoots are shredded and fermented in water or dry-packed.

Bamboo shoots are naturally high in dietary fiber and low in fat. The fermentation process enriches them with:

  • Lignans and Phenolic Compounds: These act as strong antioxidants, neutralizing free radicals in the body.
  • Phytosterols: Structurally similar to cholesterol, plant sterols compete for absorption in the small intestine, helping lower overall circulating blood cholesterol levels.

Caloric Profiles and Portioning

Because these fermented foods are vegetable- or legume-based and contain no added cooking oils, their caloric footprint is minimal. Gundruk and Khorisa contain virtually zero fat and less than 30 calories per serving, making them highly density-efficient additions to a weight loss program. Axone, being soybean-based, is higher in protein and healthy fats, yielding about 90-110 kcal per tablespoon, and should be portioned mindfully.
