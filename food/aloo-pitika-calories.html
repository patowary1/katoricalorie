Aloo Pitika Calorie Profile

Official KatoriCalorie Recipe & Nutrient Profile

In every Assamese household, comfort is spelled as Aloo Pitika (আলু পিটিকা). This simple dish—mashed boiled potatoes kneaded with raw onions, green chilies, coriander leaves, and a few drops of pungent mustard oil—is the ultimate side dish served alongside steamed rice and dal.

While Aloo Pitika is simple and highly nutritious, potatoes are high in rapid-digesting starches, giving them a high Glycemic Index (GI). However, with a few scientific adjustments, you can enjoy this local favorite while maintaining a stable glucose response and lower net calories.

Step-by-Step Glycemic-Controlled Recipe

You can significantly reduce the insulin impact of your Aloo Pitika by utilizing Retrograded Resistant Starch. Follow these steps:

  1. Boil and Chill (The Core Hack): Boil 2 medium potatoes until fully cooked.Now simply let them rest for 5 to 10 minutes to cool down slightly so they are safe to handle, then prepare for mashing immediately to retain the perfect soft texture. This cooling process recrystallizes the starches, turning digestible starch into *resistant starch* which passes into the colon without being absorbed.
  2. Knead: Remove the potatoes from the fridge and let them return to room temperature. Peel and mash them in a clean bowl.
  3. Add Fresh Herbs: Add 1 small finely chopped red onion, 1-2 green chilies, and a handful of chopped fresh coriander leaves. The raw onion provides prebiotics, and the coriander adds refreshing antioxidants. Also to increase the test of aloo pitika assamese people uses till powder(তিলৰ গুৰি).
  4. The Mustard Oil Touch: Drizzle exactly 1/2 teaspoon of raw mustard oil. The fat in the mustard oil acts as a physical barrier, slowing down gastric emptying and further reducing the glycemic response.
  5. Season: Add a pinch of salt to taste and knead the mixture together until smooth. Serve at room temperature.

Portion Control Strategies

Aloo Pitika is highly palatable, making it easy to consume larger amounts than planned. To manage your portion sizes:

  • Limit Your Serving: Restrict your intake to a 100g portion (roughly the size of a billiard ball), which keeps the net carbohydrate intake to around 20g.
  • The Meal Sequence: Always eat your protein (e.g., grilled fish or Masor Tenga) and green vegetables before eating your rice and Aloo Pitika. This slows starch absorption in the intestine.
