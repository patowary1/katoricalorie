Til Pitha Portion Control

Official KatoriCalorie Recipe & Nutrient Profile

During the celebrations of Bihu, the sweet aroma of toasted sesame seeds and glutinous rice flour fills the air in Assam. The most famous outcome of this festive cooking is the Til Pitha (তিল পিঠা)—a rolled, roasted pancake filled with sweetened black sesame seeds.

While Til Pitha is naturally gluten-free and cooked without a single drop of butter, ghee, or oil, its dry ingredient structure makes it very calorie-dense. The combination of sticky rice flour (Bora Saul) and sweet stuffing (jaggery and sesame) requires careful portion management if you are tracking your macros on a weight loss program.

Step-by-Step Preparation & Handling

Preparing the perfect Til Pitha is an art that requires patience and dry heat:

  1. Prep the Flour: Soak glutinous sticky rice (Bora Saul) for 3–4 hours, drain completely, and grind into a fine powder. Sieve it multiple times to ensure a smooth, moist texture. Keep it covered with a damp cloth so it doesn't dry out.
  2. Prepare the Filling: Roast black sesame seeds (til) until they release a nutty aroma. Grind them coarsely and mix them with grated organic sugarcane jaggery (Gur).
  3. The Griddle Technique: Heat a flat iron tawa on low heat. Place a small handful of the moist rice powder onto the tawa and press it gently with your fingers or a clean cotton cloth to form a thin, circular disc. Do not use oil or water.
  4. Assemble and Roll: Quickly place a tablespoon of the sweet sesame-jaggery mixture in the center. As the rice flour binds together due to the steam from the heat, fold the sides over the filling to form a neat, cylindrical roll.
  5. Cool: Remove the rolled pitha from the tawa and let it cool. It should be crisp on the outside and soft, sweet, and nutty on the inside.

Portion Control Strategies

At 85 calories per small pitha, eating three or four in one sitting can quickly add up to 340 calories—equivalent to a full meal, but without the corresponding long-term satiety. To enjoy Til Pitha on a calorie-restricted diet:

  • Limit to One: Enjoy a single pitha as an afternoon snack accompanied by a cup of unsweetened black tea or local red tea (Lal Sa).
  • Protein Pairing: Eat your pitha after a protein-dense meal. The protein and fiber in your stomach will slow the digestion of the simple sugars in the jaggery, minimizing insulin spikes.
  • Track Diligently: Ensure you log each piece on your KatoriCalorie interactive dashboard to stay within your daily caloric allowance.
